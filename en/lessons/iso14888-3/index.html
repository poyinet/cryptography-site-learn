By the end, you can explain what “Digital signatures with appendix — Part 3: Discrete logarithm based mechanisms” solves, which assumption it relies on, and at least one boundary where it fails.

Standard : Digital signatures with appendix — Part 3: Discrete logarithm based mechanisms (基于离散对数的数字签名). ISO/IEC 14888-3:2018 specifies digital signatures with appendix based on the discrete logarithm problem.…

为什么值得读

Putting 14/15 mechanisms together reveals “one equation, different groups and witness functions”. DSA/ECDSA/SM2/EdDSA are not four unrelated algorithms: each starts from a random K and private X and uses a verifiable equation to bind R and S to the message.…

边界与攻击面

Nonce reuse is fatal: two messages signed with the same K let an attacker solve the two S equations for the private key. K bias, low-entropy RNG and fault injection can also leak the key. EC-DSA, EC-GDSA, EC-RDSA and EC-FSDSA can be vulnerable to key substitution: an attacker finds two distinct public keys and one signature valid for both; the standard suggests adding the public key to the signed message.…
